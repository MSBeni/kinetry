# Leading your first team meeting

A three-part primer for people stepping into their first team-lead role. Each part can be turned into its own short video.

## Prepare with a purpose

Before you send the invite, decide what this first meeting is for. It is not a status update. It is the moment your team forms its first impression of how you lead.

Write down one sentence that describes what you want the team to leave with. Keep it simple, for example: "We know how we will work together for the next month."

Then gather three inputs:

- What each person is working on right now, in their own words
- One thing that is going well for the team, and one thing that is slowing it down
- Any decisions that are already made and should not be reopened

Send a short agenda a day ahead so nobody walks in guessing.

## Run the room

Open by saying why you called the meeting and what you hope to leave with. Then hand the floor over quickly. Your job in the first meeting is mostly to listen.

Go around the room and ask each person to share what they are working on and one thing that would make their work easier. Write what you hear somewhere everyone can see.

Watch for three patterns:

- The quiet person who has not spoken yet. Invite them in by name, gently.
- The loudest topic that takes over. Park it with a clear promise to return to it.
- Agreement that comes too fast. Ask, "What would make this fail?"

Keep to time. Ending a few minutes early builds more trust than a perfect agenda.

## Follow through

The meeting only counts if something changes afterwards. Within one day, send a short recap: what you heard, what you decided, and who owns each next step.

Pick one small problem the team raised and fix it within the week. Visible follow-through on something small tells people that speaking up works.

At the next meeting, open by naming what changed since last time. Then ask what still is not working. Over a few weeks this rhythm turns into the way your team talks.
